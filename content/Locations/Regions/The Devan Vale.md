# Description
The setting of the adventure is a thinly populated human frontier known as Devan Vale and the surrounding wilderness. The vale stretches almost 250 miles east to west and averages about 70 miles north to south. Several mountain ranges and dense forests form the vale’s borders.

### CLIMATE AND TERRAIN
Summers are hot and humid (although punctuated by the occasional intense thunderstorm), and winters are cold and rainy. Large stretches of the area are quite fertile, and the vale is riddled with brooks, streams, and rivers. The forests that stretch across most of the vale’s northern reaches are dense and dark, home to a wide variety of deadly monsters.

##### Weather
We’ve set this adventure in the middle of a hot, wet summer. The default temperature for the season is hot (85° to 110° F) during the day and warm (60° to 85° F) at night. If you’re using Table 3–23: Random Weather (DMG 94), then a heat wave makes the temperature for the day severely hot, and poses some amount of heat danger (DMG 303). If you don’t want to bother with heat dangers, don’t roll on the table; the weather just stays hot or warm every day. Occasionally tell the players just how sweaty and dusty their characters are, especially those in armor or bulky clothing, in order to set the mood. This is the dry season in Elsir Vale, so rainfall is infrequent— but when it comes down, it really comes down. Thunderstorms and tornados are fairly common. See the rules for fighting in wind and precipitation (DMG 94–95). 

##### Clear Terrain
In general, areas on the map that don’t clearly possess a terrain type consist of a mix of grasslands, hills, light woods, and brushland. If you need to know the heroes’ immediate surroundings, roll d% and consult the table below. 

| d%     | Terrain            |
| ------ | ------------------ |
| 01-15  | Forest (sparse)    |
| 16-20  | Forest (medium)    |
| 21-30  | Plains (fields)    |
| 31-60  | Plains (grassland) |
| 61-80  | Brush              |
| 81-100 | Hills (gentle)     |

##### Settled Lands
Land in the vicinity of a village or town is cleared farmland. Scattered homesteads and farmhouses lie amid broad fields, with the occasional hillock or copse of woodland. The closer to the village, the closer together the outlying farms and homesteads. Treat settled lands as plains (farm fields).
