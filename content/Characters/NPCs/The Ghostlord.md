#### Description

A ghastly elven mage with wispy white hair and dark, sunken eyes that glow with a ghostly luminescence, wearing faded, high-collared silks of blue and green, draped with robes once black with gold embroidery, now worn down to a ashen grey. Sharp, gaunt features, and pale, nearly translucent skin pulled taught give it an almost skeletal appearance, especially the torn skin on either side revealing the gleam of exposed teeth.

#### History

Born at the close of the elven golden age, he grew to adulthood revering the living wilderness and honoring the memories of those who came before.  Extremely intelligent, he probed deeply into the natural cycle asking questions like, "Where do the dead go? Do they remember? What is lost when we cross the Veil?" This led him to seek answers at the Tower of the Ancestors, where he learned to

- commune with the honored dead
- preserve their legacies
- perform the rites of burial and consecration
- guide restless spirits to the Great River
- and even to walk the paths of the Underworld.

He knew about elven reincarnation, but learned that most mortal souls slip into reverie and forgetfulness unless propitiated by certain rites to remind them of physical life (ala the Odyssey) while only a chosen few escape oblivion to enter Heaven or Hell. Ultimately mortal souls left to wander the Underworld are swept up by the Great River and absorbed or dissolved into the fabric of the cosmos, from which new souls can be forged. This is all part of the great circle of life.

Pitying mortals, he studied the arts of calling and binding mortal souls, against the warnings of his teachers, but to his horror each soul he bound eventually became bitter and forgetful, fading to a mere shade of their former selves, as though the entropy of the Underworld could not be stopped. When his peers discovered his experiments, he was banished from the Tower, though he did not journey far...

He sought guidance from an ancient sphinx guarding a place of great power in the heart of Danuvia's rolling plains, a stone spur of the nearby mountains riddled with caves, within which lay a Well of tremendous magical power.  Sadly here he received the same answers: the cycle is immutable, all things must end and renewed in new forms; consciousness is a gift to be cherished, and eventually relinquished. Unable to accept this, the Ghostlord flew into a rage, accusing the sphinx of jealously guarding her secrets, demanding she provide what he sought. A fierce battle ensued and the sphinx was slain, sending the Ghostlord into despair and remorse. He attempted to revive her, but could not, and in desperation clawed at the dark power of the Underworld to reanimate her flesh, desecrating that holy place. He made a vow to take up the sphinx's stewardship of the Well, and even had stone carved to resemble her likeness.

From this unholy sanctum, he threw himself into is research, seeking ever and with increasing desperation for power and knowledge to bind and make eternal the souls of the living. Drawing power from the Well he opened forbidden gates to worlds beyond our own, even those beyond the Veil. He walked the silent paths of the Underworld, and even journeyed into the darkness of the Pitt - no power was too vile to seek out, no creature too evil to propitiate - until he discovered legends of the Baleful King, a powerful demon who once sought to rule a world of perfectly obedient and eternal undead servants. He journeyed even unto the very throne of Thanatos, the Lord of Bones, where he learned the rites to gain immortality through lichdom.

Completing this rite came at a dreadful cost: many sacrifices would be required. The Ghostlord sought out a warlike tribe causing trouble in the region, hoping to visit justice upon them, and slew an entire village in offering to Thanatos.  Their reprisal was unforeseen - they discovered his home and massacred the peaceful villages around the mighty sphinx statue while he underwent his transformation. Enraged, he stormed forth in fury, armed with terrible new power, and annihilated the invaders. The slain villagers he called back and bound as Unseen Servants with ghostly appearances, or specters if they were strong enough, while the invaders became wights or skeletons doomed to serve him until they are destroyed utterly.
